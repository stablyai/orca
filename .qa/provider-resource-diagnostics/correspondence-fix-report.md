## Functional correctness

The old owner updated hook metadata before opening a reported transcript, retaining the old transcript when the session stayed the same and the new object differed or failed to open. Queries then attributed the old object to the new hook. Read the independent review MD/JSON and its synthetic invalidation TS/JSON in full.

The execution owner now replaces one observation containing the hook and optional transcript on every accepted report. Queries snapshot that observation and check its identity across asynchronous reads. New reports immediately remove current correspondence even during an in-flight query/probe, with only the latest bounded pending probe retained. A successful read fills only its own still-current observation; failed, missing, null, relative, unsupported, non-file, capacity-refused and superseded reads cannot restore stale evidence. Same-session distinct objects and same-path replacements acquire their own correspondence after a new hook. Same-object aliases preserve their object observation ID while a descriptor still pins the object; resumed sessions get a new session correlation. Prior descriptors are closed after replacement settles or queued work is cancelled, and are never independently queryable historical evidence.

## Architectural fit

This removes the diagnostic stale-join mechanism at the shared native/relay store boundary, rather than adding another session/path guard. Replacing the entire diagnostic approach with authority/admission machinery would exceed the requested evidence-only contract. The broader invocation problem remains: placement/profile claims do not establish execution-host resource authority. Every resource verdict remains `unverifiable`, separate from PTY `live` / `unverifiable` / `exited`; no admission, stop, recovery or resume decisions changed.

Opt-in, 128-record retention, eight probes, one probe per record, latest pending hook per record, finite 15-minute owner enrollment/retention and epoch checks remain. At most one previous transcript descriptor per transitioning record is pinned during replacement; pending cancellation and disposal release it. No historical inventory, polling or unbounded fanout was added. Filesystem operations still have no cancellation deadline: finite record/probe counts do not prove latency bounds on hung filesystems.
